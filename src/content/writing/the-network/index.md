Most players only notice the door exists on the day it shuts behind them.

**Access is the fastest-depreciating asset you own.** The money can be protected. The story can be built. The access has a clock on it that starts running the moment you are no longer relevant, and it runs quickly. Which means the work is simple to state and hard to do. Build the relationships while the door is open, long before you need to walk through it.

Start with the distinction that everything else depends on. A contact is a number in a phone. A relationship is someone who would take your call in five years, when you are no longer useful to them. Almost every player confuses the two. They have a phone full of contacts collected at events and dinners, and they mistake the volume for a network. Then the career ends, they start reaching out, and they discover that a contact who owed you nothing and heard from you only when you needed something is not a relationship at all.

> You build the second kind by being useful first, when you need nothing in return. The transactional networker asks for something in the first meeting. The compounding networker gives for a year and asks in the second. An introduction between two people who should know each other. Attention on something someone is building. A connection made with no scoreboard attached. Founders in particular remember exactly who helped them before they were worth helping, and that memory pays out for decades.

This connects directly back to the second education. For you, the network is not only access and deals. It is the university. The room is where you learn, and the people in it are the faculty. So building the network does double duty in a way it does not for most people. Every relationship you build teaches you something now and compounds into access you will want later. That is a rate of return on relationships that few other paths offer, and it is available to you because of who you are while you play.

Build it around the right thing. Founders, operators, investors, creators, people outside sport, gathered around the purpose from Part 2 rather than around deals. This matters more than it sounds. A network assembled around deals dies the moment the deals stop, because it was never a network, only a series of transactions. A network assembled around an interest keeps producing for the rest of your life, because the interest does not expire when the contract does.

The method is short. Keep one list of people you would want to know in ten years, chosen for who you would want beside you later rather than who is useful now. Give first, consistently, with no scoreboard. Meet in person wherever you can, because both the signal you send and the learning you take are higher in a room than in a message. And tend the few relationships that connect you to everyone else deliberately, because a small number of people are the difference between a network that opens the whole world to you and a phone full of numbers that go nowhere.

**Most players spend their access, one meeting at a time, until it runs out. The ones who last invest it, quietly, into people who will still pick up the phone years after they stop being useful. Build it before you need it, because it cannot be built after.**
